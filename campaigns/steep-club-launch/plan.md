# The Steep Club — waitlist launch plan

Campaign slug: `steep-club-launch`
Brief received: 29 October 2026
Plan covers: 2 November 2026 to 29 November 2026 (four weeks)
Status: plan only. No emails written yet.

---

## 1. Goal

**300 waitlist members start a paid Steep Club subscription between 2 November 2026 and 29 November 2026.**

That is 15% of a 2,000-person waitlist. The number to watch is **started subscriptions**, not opens or clicks.

---

## 2. Audience

About **2,000 people** who joined the Steep Club waitlist. They already raised a hand, so this is a warm list, not a cold one.

Three groups are named in the brief:

| Group | Who they are | What they need from us |
|---|---|---|
| Tea-curious beginners | People who like the idea of tea but do not know where to start | Permission to be new at this. A simple first step. |
| Gift buyers | People looking for a gift, often for someone else | Clear gifting facts: how a gift works, when it arrives, what it costs. |
| Daily tea drinkers | People who already drink tea every day | Something new to try in a habit they already have. Depth, not basics. |

**Group sizes are not known yet.** The brief gives the total only. If the list splits evenly, each group is about 667 people, and 15% of each group is about 100 subscriptions. Use that split as a planning assumption, not a fact. Confirm the real counts before the first send (see section 10).

Everyone on this list chose to hear from us. That is an advantage, and it is also a duty: the consent record, the unsubscribe link and the send frequency all have to be right (see section 9).

---

## 3. Positioning

**One idea to leave behind:**

> The Steep Club is a small monthly box of tea, chosen for you, so there is always something new in the cupboard and a quiet moment to look forward to.

That is it. Not a health story. Not a bargain. Not a life change. A good cup, a new tea, a small ritual you can keep.

Two supporting ideas, used lightly:

- **You are early.** The waitlist gets in before anyone else. That is a real benefit and needs no invented deadline.
- **You are not signing a contract with your personality.** You can try the club, and you can stop. (Only say this once the pause and cancel policy is confirmed — see section 10.)

---

## 4. Key messages

Three to five messages, each tied to a group. Every one is inside the brand voice: warm, plain, curious.

1. **For tea-curious beginners:** "You do not need to know about tea to start. The first box is a simple place to begin." — No jargon, no tasting vocabulary test, no pressure.
2. **For daily tea drinkers:** "You already have your daily cup. The club gives you something new to try in it." — Speaks to a habit they already have, not a habit they must build.
3. **For gift buyers:** "A box is a small gift that arrives again next month." — Only usable once the gift option and shipping dates are confirmed.
4. **For everyone:** "The waitlist gets in first." — True because they are the waitlist. No countdown and no urgency line.
5. **For everyone:** "A quiet cup, a new tea, and a small ritual you can keep. That is The Steep Club." — The line that carries the campaign. It is the on-voice example from `brand/voice.md`.

**Words and claims we will not use:** the avoid list and the claims section in `brand/voice.md` apply to every line of this campaign. No invented price, discount, testimonial, customer count or deadline. No health claims of any kind.

---

## 5. Offer and constraints

**What is offered**

- First access to The Steep Club for the waitlist.
- A monthly box of tea, sent to the subscriber.
- A simple way to start, and a simple way to stop (policy to be confirmed).
- Gifting, **if** gift subscriptions are ready in time (to be confirmed). Any gifting paragraph is held back until this is answered.

**What is not offered**

- No discount, no free trial, no free shipping promise, no referral bonus.
- No Black Friday or Cyber Monday promotion. We do not invent a sale. 27 November 2026 falls inside this campaign and is deliberately left empty.
- No guarantee, no ranking claim, no health or wellness claim.
- No countdown timer and no fake deadline. The only deadline stated is the real one: the club is open to the waitlist this month.

**Constraints**

- **Budget: small.** One email service provider, one designer-free plain-text-and-simple-HTML template set. No paid media.
- **Channel: email only.** No paid ads, no social, no SMS, no print, no influencer work.
- **Send volume:** about 2,000 contacts, 4 sends across four weeks. Each person receives up to four emails, one a week. Later group versions change the opening line, not the frequency.
- **Facts we do not have yet:** price, billing cadence, box contents, shipping regions, December shipping cutoff, gift rules, pause and cancel policy. None of these may appear in an email until confirmed in writing.

---

## 6. Channel plan

**Used**

- **Email.** The only outbound channel. Four sends, one a week, sent from the studio's email service provider.
- **The existing signup page on the website.** Where interested people start a subscription. This is the destination, not a new channel. No new landing pages and no new tools.

**Deliberately excluded**

- Paid ads of any kind (search, social, display, newsletter sponsorship).
- Organic social posts and influencer seeding.
- SMS and push notifications.
- Print, direct mail and inserts.
- Referral and affiliate programmes.
- A Black Friday or Cyber Monday discount send.
- Re-marketing or retargeting audiences.

**Why:** the brief says email only with a small budget. A warm 2,000-person waitlist does not need paid reach. Adding a channel would add cost, consent questions and message clutter for no clear gain in four weeks.

## 7. Email calendar

All dates are real ISO dates in November 2026. Every date was checked against its weekday.

| Date | Weekday | Audience group | Purpose | Email | Subject angle |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2026-11-02 | Monday | All waitlist (all three groups) | Open the club to the waitlist and set the tone | 01 — "The club is open" | The Steep Club is open to the waitlist first |
| 2026-11-10 | Tuesday | All waitlist (emphasis: tea-curious beginners) | Explain the shape of the club without stating unconfirmed facts | 02 — "How the box works" | The shape of the club, without the guesswork |
| 2026-11-17 | Tuesday | All waitlist (emphasis: daily tea drinkers and gift buyers) | Show what a first box feels like, without inventing gifting facts | 03 — "What arrives" | A new tea for your everyday cup |
| 2026-11-24 | Tuesday | All non-subscribers | A calm final invitation. No fake deadline | 04 — "One more cup" | The club is still open. Here is the simple way in |

**Notes on the calendar**

- **One sequence, four emails.** Every email goes to the full eligible waitlist, one a week. The three customer groups get their own versions later: same structure, subject, link and footer, with a different opening line and one example.
- **Week 1, 2–8 November:** announce the club on Monday and set the tone.
- **Week 2, 9–15 November:** explain the shape of the club without stating unconfirmed facts. It leans toward beginners. It must not state a price, a billing date or a box list.
- **Week 3, 16–22 November:** show what a first box feels like. It leans toward daily drinkers and gift buyers, but it must not describe a gifting flow that is not confirmed.
- **Week 4, 23–29 November:** one calm close on 24 November. Nothing on 27 November. No discount, no countdown.
- **Gifting:** if gift subscriptions are not confirmed before 10 November, email 03 stays a box-and-tea email and says nothing about gifts. Gifting gets its own campaign later.
- **Throttle:** send in batches so the provider does not push 2,000 messages at once. Keep the same send time each week.
- **Suppression:** anyone who subscribes is removed from later sends. Anyone who unsubscribes is removed from everything.
- **Day of week:** no sends on Saturday or Sunday. Every send is on a Monday or Tuesday, at 10:00 local time.

## 8. Metrics

Targets are for the whole four weeks, measured per send and cumulatively.

| Stage | Metric | Target | Why this number |
|---|---|---|---|
| Delivery | Delivered rate | 98% or higher | A warm, opt-in list should deliver cleanly. Below 95% means a list or authentication problem. |
| Delivery | Bounce rate | Under 2% | Hard bounces above 2% on an opt-in list point to stale or invalid addresses. |
| Delivery | Spam complaint rate | Under 0.1% per send | Above 0.1% risks the domain, not just this campaign. |
| Attention | Open rate, announcement email | 40% or higher | This is a waitlist that asked to hear from us. |
| Attention | Open rate, later emails | 25% or higher | Later sends are follow-ups, not news. |
| Interest | Click rate, announcement email | 8% or higher | Clicking is the first real signal of intent. |
| Interest | Click rate, later emails | 4% or higher | Follow-ups convert a smaller, more decided group. |
| Intent | Start-a-subscription rate from clicks | 30% or higher | If clicks are high and starts are low, the signup page is the problem, not the email. |
| Conversion | Subscriptions started, week 1 (by 8 Nov) | 60 | Early movers. |
| Conversion | Subscriptions started, cumulative by 15 Nov | 150 | The half-way check. Below 100 means the offer or the facts are unclear. |
| Conversion | Subscriptions started, cumulative by 22 Nov | 225 | If this is behind, week 4 needs a clearer, not louder, final email. |
| Conversion | Subscriptions started, cumulative by 29 Nov | **300** | The goal. 15% of 2,000. |
| Per group | Subscriptions by group | About 100 each, if groups are about 667 | Planning assumption only. Confirm real counts first. |
| Health | Unsubscribe rate per send | Under 0.5% | Above this means frequency or relevance is wrong. |
| Health | Replies and questions | Read every one | The clearest signal of what the emails are failing to say. |

**Review points:** end of week 1 and end of week 2. If starts are under half the cumulative target at either point, change the message, not the volume. Do not add extra sends to catch up.

## 9. Risks and compliance

**Consent and list hygiene**

- Confirm how every one of the 2,000 addresses was collected, and that the signup made clear they would hear about the club by email. Keep the record of that consent on file.
- Remove role addresses, obvious duplicates and anyone who has already unsubscribed before the first send.
- Do not buy, rent, swap or append addresses. The whole plan assumes this list only.

**Unsubscribe and preference**

- One-click unsubscribe in every email, working without a login. Honor it immediately, across all future sends.
- Honest sender name and a real reply-to address that a person reads.
- Include a postal address if the business has one, or the provider's registered details. Check the local rules that apply to the business.

**Claims**

- No health claims. The claims section of `brand/voice.md` applies to subject lines, preview text and body copy, in any wording.
- No invented price, discount, testimonial, customer count, deadline or guarantee.
- The brand's avoid-list in `brand/voice.md` applies to subject lines, preview text and body copy alike.
- Every email ends with the exact sign-off: `Warm cups,` / `The Kettlebird Tea team`.

**Deliverability**

- Authenticate the sending domain: SPF, DKIM and DMARC in place before 2 November.
- Warm the domain if it is new. Four sends in four weeks to 2,000 people is modest, but only if the domain has some history.
- Plain, simple HTML with a real text version. No image-only emails, no link shorteners, no misleading subject lines.
- Watch bounces and complaints after every send, not at the end of the month.

**Commercial and factual**

- Price, billing cadence, box contents, shipping regions and the December cutoff must be confirmed in writing before any email states them. Email 02 deliberately does not state them and sends readers to the club page instead.
- The pause and cancel policy must be confirmed before any email says "you can stop any time".
- Gift subscriptions: if they are not ready, gifting is not mentioned in any email. Do not describe a gifting flow that does not exist.
- No Black Friday discount. If someone asks for one, that is a decision for the owner, not a copy change.

**Things the team must confirm before the first send**

1. Price and billing cadence.
2. What is in the first box.
3. Where we ship, and the December shipping cutoff.
4. Whether gift subscriptions exist, and how they work.
5. Pause and cancel policy.
6. Real group sizes.
7. Who reads replies, and how fast.

---

## 10. Open questions

Only things a human must decide. Nothing here can be invented safely.

1. **What is the price and billing cadence?** Monthly or otherwise. No email can invite a subscription without it, and the signup page needs it too.
2. **What is in the first box?** We cannot describe a box we have not defined.
3. **Do gift subscriptions exist for this launch?** If yes, how does a gift work, and can the buyer choose a start date? This decides whether email 03 may mention gifting at all.
4. **What is the December shipping cutoff?** Gift buyers need a real date. Without it, email 03 must not describe gift timing.
5. **What is the pause and cancel policy?** Needed before we say "you can stop when you like".
6. **How big is each of the three groups, really?** The plan assumes an even split for its per-group targets. Confirm and update section 8.
7. **Where did the waitlist consent come from, and is it recorded?** Needed before the first send, for legal and for deliverability.
8. **Who is the sender, and who reads replies?** A named person or team address, not a no-reply.
9. **Is the business in a region with specific email marketing rules we must follow?** If so, name them and check the footer requirements.
10. **Should gifting get its own campaign once the details exist, rather than one paragraph inside email 03?** A separate push keeps this launch clean and gives gift buyers the facts they need.
